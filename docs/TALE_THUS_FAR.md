# The Tale Thus Far — an earned campaign ballad

**Design saved 7 October 2026.** The owner approved a rhyming, old-fable retelling of the story already experienced, built up through play and excerpted on loading screens. This document develops that direction; the title, exact verses, presentation, conditions and delivery packages below are **authoring proposals**, not implemented features or recording-approved text.

Read with [campaign canon](CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md), [chapter structure](DUNGEON_CHAPTER_PLANS.md), the [current English dialogue bank](dialogue/en/README.md), [UI/loading reference](design/ui/README.md) and [Bellwether life](BELLWETHER_VILLAGE_LIFE.md). The ballad does not replace ordinary, naturally spoken dialogue, practical objectives or accessible factual recaps. It is a separate storytelling voice, not the silent protagonist speaking, a newly canonised bard, or an omniscient character inside the world.

## 1. Creative shape

A tale that sounds old even while the player is making it: simple four-line stanzas, mostly four stresses per line, paired rhymes, concrete things before abstract lore. Stone, rope, shutters, bread, bells and a waiting chair make stronger anchors than a succession of proper names. Let each chapter have a little wonder, then let later knowledge alter the meaning of remembered images. Avoid mock-medieval grammar, heroic titles the player has not earned and a rhyme at the expense of truth.

The accumulating refrain is the contrast between **what can be carried away and what is worth returning to**. It begins with a light and a way out; Bellwether adds shelter; the dungeons complicate treasure; the ending earns a homecoming. Do not promise that the player feels at home, chose generosity or rejected wealth. The player may keep ordinary treasure and still receive the approved hopeful ending.

Earlier earned stanzas stay readable. Later verses answer them rather than silently rewriting history: the lantern's claimed crown becomes a borrowed name; apparently protective custody becomes legible as imprisonment. Early narration reports what a voice *claimed*, never certifies its account as fact. The ballad cannot be a source of advance clues that the player has not actually encountered.

## 2. Where the player meets it

- **First introduction:** after the collapse and Kit's actual wall-panel call, offer the first earned stanza at a safe existing presentation opportunity. No pre-game prophecy, extra cutscene, forced camera or fabricated quote from Kit. If the call was skipped, the voice-containing stanza remains unavailable until that event is genuinely established; an event-only variant could be written later.
- **Returning player:** an optional “The Tale Thus Far” entry beside the save's normal summary. Show a short selection of the most recent earned verses, with “Read the tale” for the full text. Do not demand a reading pause, infer absence from wall-clock time, or obscure location/objective/checkpoint information.
- **Loading screens:** select a self-contained two-line couplet from earned text only, with a small spinner. The archived progress bar is superseded. Do not add loading transitions to create reading opportunities, lengthen loading, wait for a recital, or flash through verses during fast loads. If there is insufficient stable display time, omit the verse; the persistent reading view supplies it later.
- **Persistent reading view:** unlocked stanzas only, in the order experienced. Abbey and Foundry exchange positions according to the save's actual path. No greyed future chapter names, unrevealed thumbnails, total-stanza completion counter or locked ending heading. Optional choice codas sit beside their relevant chapter.
- **Completion:** the player can reread the assembled, save-specific ballad after the playable return. It need not be recited in one sitting. The normal epilogue remains playable.

Use scalable text, portrait/landscape safe areas, generous contrast and static presentation under reduced motion. Do not rely on audio or colour. Verse reflow, screen-reader reading order and localization need review; do not shrink text to preserve an English line width.

## 3. Candidate verse bank

**All text below is draft English; recording approval = false.** `TALE-DRAFT-*` are design-reference labels only, not production dialogue IDs. Conditions are semantic requirements to map to actual accepted state later. A location visit, seal count or quest availability alone does not establish knowledge. Each stanza requires *all* the listed observations/events in the current save. No automatic unlock by milestone number.

### TALE-DRAFT-01 — after collapse and the heard wall-panel call

The stone gave way; the dust lay deep.  
Below, the dark had much to keep.  
Yet through the wall a friend was near;  
His voice outlasted falling fear.

This is narration, not altered Kit dialogue or a claim that Kit sees the player.

### TALE-DRAFT-02 — after lich defeat and lantern claim

The keeper fell; the chamber stilled.  
A waiting lamp the darkness filled.  
A light to lift, a flame to tend;  
The buried path was not the end.

“Fell” describes the approved defeat, not a new animation or fate beyond canon. This stanza does not yet identify the keeper as a jailer or the lamp as a prison.

### TALE-DRAFT-03 — after completed rope rescue and forest reunion

A rope was cast; a hand held fast.  
The roots received the tomb's outcast.  
Above the leaves the moon shone white;  
The road ran on through silver night.

Unlock only after the actual rescue, not merely approaching an opening. No daylight, dawn, curse lifting or knowingly delayed rescue is implied.

### TALE-DRAFT-04 — after village arrival and experienced welcome

Beyond the trees a hearth burned red;  
A place was found for boots and bread.  
The road could wait outside the door;  
For now, the world asked nothing more.

The welcome/food staging must actually support this wording; otherwise hold or revise it. This is respite, not a promise that all objectives disappear or food must become a survival meter.

### TALE-DRAFT-05 — after Kit's injury and town recovery base are established

The road had asked a friend's delay;  
He could not walk each mile away.  
Yet when the traveller crossed the square,  
A place beside his friend was there.

Only the general approved injury/recovery arc is used. Exact cause, severity, body part and recovery duration remain undecided. Kit is neither abandoned nor magically present in later dungeons. “Traveller” is the narrator's term, not a player name or voiced identity.

### TALE-DRAFT-06 — after hearing the lantern's royal claim and three-seal request

The lantern spoke of crown and throne,  
Of faithless guards and years alone.  
It named three seals, a hidden store,  
And promised peace beyond its door.

Report an account told, not objective history. Use only once the actual accepted speech establishes all four ideas; “peace” is a draft paraphrase of ending the suffering, not a new promise added to the canonical spoken bank.

### TALE-DRAFT-07 — Abbey: after reflector lesson, Bellkeeper victory, seal and plunder evidence

Beneath the lake the old bells lay;  
A borrowed beam revealed the way.  
One seal was won, one truth made plain:  
The royal gifts were others' pain.

Requires the plunder revelation, not just boss defeat. Works first or second. It does not reveal the Abbey flood's cause, kill the living claimant, or assume a relic choice.

### TALE-DRAFT-08 — Foundry: after stand lesson, Master of Coin victory, seal and binding evidence

A light stood fast while feet moved free;  
The furnace yielded up its key.  
Yet death had brought no end to toil;  
The king's old bargain claimed the spoil.

“Key” is figurative for this chapter's seal; do not add an inventory key. Requires evidence of the royal bargain and soldiers' continued service. It does not identify the voice or make the Abbey necessary first.

### TALE-DRAFT-09 — both peer seals earned and their shared gate requirement understood

Two roads returned to one small square;  
Two seals were carried safely there.  
Beyond the roofs, above the plain,  
The higher road could call again.

Require both completed returns if retaining “returned”; otherwise do not unlock yet. The stanza describes readiness for the higher route, not an already opened Court gate or newly known treasury location.

### TALE-DRAFT-10 — Court: after aperture use, keeper defeat and understood identity evidence

A narrow light undid the ward;  
The glass gave back a falsehood's shard.  
The voice had worn another's name;  
The king had helped to cage its flame.

Unlock only after the player actually receives/inspects the approved evidence. Neither merely defeating the keeper nor collecting a seal substitutes for understanding it. No keeper death, optional garden-date clue or precise speech interval is assumed.

### TALE-DRAFT-11 — after the shared bond/release risk is understood

One bond held fast the dead and foe;  
To loose the one let both things go.  
No kindly word could change the cost,  
Nor leave uncounted those still lost.

This is not an instruction to choose an unapproved ending. Preserve the genuine good of freeing the soldiers and the dangerous consequence. Gate separately from identity truth if the bond explanation is learned later. It must not overstate a still-unapproved ritual mechanism.

### TALE-DRAFT-12 — after the treasury is actually opened and seen

The vault held more than tales had told:  
The promised wealth was weight and gold.  
No empty jest, no painted prize;  
The hoard lay real before their eyes.

“They/their” refers to the player neutrally, not Kit standing in the treasury. Retain the treasure's genuine material desirability. No distribution, relic identity or numerical value is invented.

### TALE-DRAFT-13 — after the ritual actually frees the bound dead and entity

The bond gave way; the long watch ceased.  
The dead went free; so did the beast.  
The voice no longer begged release;  
Its freedom had not purchased peace.

“Beast” is an epithet, not an approved animal body or entity model. Review against final encounter staging before adoption. Do not play while competing with essential combat cues. Exact ritual/counterplay remains decision-blocked.

### TALE-DRAFT-14 — after entity defeat and confirmed release outcome

The borrowed crown could rule no more;  
No oath recalled the dead to war.  
The treasure waited to be borne;  
No soldier stood to sound the horn.

The horn is figurative, not a newly required instrument or absent-NPC proof. Gate on the ending's actual accepted outcome, not an arbitrary boss health flag. Final encounter details and losses remain open; revise after those decisions.

### TALE-DRAFT-15 — after the playable return and Kit reunion in Bellwether

The road led back; the door stood wide.  
A friend still kept a place inside.  
And after gold and grief and stone,  
There stood a light that shone for home.

Requires the actual return/welcome staging. No claim that Kit escorted the finale, fully healed, or that the player surrendered all treasure. “Home” expresses the tale's invitation, not a forced spoken feeling.

### Optional TALE-DRAFT-16R — verified named relic actually returned

A thing once counted with the gold  
Was handed back for hands to hold.  
One claim was heard, one debt made small;  
It did not settle debts for all.

Use only once the living claim is verified and handover completed. Do not name the object or claimant until approved. Recovered-then-returned can use this same terminal coda; sold/kept/deferred receive **no invented moral verdict or coda** until their consequences and wording are approved. Never show mutually incompatible choice stanzas together.

## 4. Reveal, choice and save contract

1. Store earned narrative events/knowledge and actual choice results in the campaign save, independent of UI screens and world-zone lifetimes. Map these semantic requirements to validated source flags; the labels here are not new runtime fields by fiat.
2. Evaluate eligibility when adding a verse and again before displaying any queued excerpt. Use an allow-list of earned stanza IDs for the selected save. Never sample the entire authoring file and merely hide a heading.
3. Persist the acquisition order of the peer chapters, without depending on wall-clock time. The main sequence follows experienced history; the “both” bridge appears only after both qualifying events. A player returning from the second chapter before reading its evidence gets no premature revelation.
4. New game, different save slot, profile change or restoring an earlier checkpoint must not inherit future text from a global UI cache. Earlier-checkpoint behaviour follows the actual restored canonical progress. No cross-save reveal memory.
5. Skipping VO does not establish an unheard optional clue. Essential evidence may be supplied by the accepted accessible text/inspection fallback; only completed delivery establishes knowledge. If an old save has no trustworthy evidence history, omit uncertain verses and retain ordinary factual progress recap. Do not reconstruct knowledge from dungeon count alone.
6. A sold relic later recovered/returned must produce the current truthful optional coda; no completed charitable verse during sale preview or cancelled handover. Preserve the historical core tale, but replace/remove choice-sensitive optional text when the authoritative state genuinely changes.
7. Keep earned verse distinct from “already displayed.” Rereading is always available; a loading-screen excerpt should not consume a scene, item or quest reward. Interrupted screens must not advance the tale.

## 5. Loading excerpt selection

Default to a recent, self-contained couplet; occasionally revisit an older earned one. Good candidates include 03 lines 3–4 (moonlit road), 04 lines 1–2 (welcome), 07 lines 3–4 (plunder, only after evidence), and 10 lines 3–4 (identity truth, only after evidence). Never combine halves from different stanzas or separate a claim qualifier from the claim it qualifies.

Suppress spoilers in filenames/UI metadata exposed to the player. The authoring document contains the whole campaign; shipped player-facing search/accessibility strings must follow the same earned-state filtering as visible text. Unavailable chapters in a partial release remain unavailable; this feature must not advertise future content as playable.

## 6. Voice, localization and bounded delivery

Text-first prototype. A later optional recital voice may sound like an old tale being remembered: warm, measured and intimate, without a trailer voice or forced singing. Speaker identity, casting, accent, music accompaniment and recording remain unapproved. Do not assign the voice to Kit or imply that an existing actor has performed it. No paid generation is authorised by this plan.

If voiced later, reuse offline dialogue playback, separate dialogue volume, subtitles, skip/replay and interruption priorities. Never hold a loading screen until VO finishes. Normal conversation, warning and combat priorities supersede decorative recitation. Localize meaning, earned-state gates and emotional intent before exact rhyme; each language may require different line breaks/meter and independently aligned timings. Review original clean text and performance instructions separately.

- **1.7 candidate:** text/read-view and save-state proof only if accepted at that milestone's scope gate; just already experienced tomb/rescue/forest material. No town verses at the lookout.
- **1.8 candidate:** add actual arrival, Kit continuity and approved first-voice observations, alongside [village life](BELLWETHER_VILLAGE_LIFE.md).
- **Later chapters:** Abbey/Foundry either order; Court only later; treasury/ending verses remain review-dependent until detailed finale decisions exist. Proposed 1.9/1.10/1.11 production packages do not dictate play order.
