# Mobile RT source census (static)

Scope: read-only shader/C++ call-path census for proposal discussion, checked in worktree `horde-1.6.1-engineering-pass` at `9623d5b`; relevant files below have no diff from accepted source `71cb366`. These are source-derived eligible-site ceilings, **not** measured per-frame or per-pixel query counts. No device, GPU, or ADB profiling was done.

## Include and direct-light paths

`minimal.rgen:7` includes `include/rt_frame.glsl`; `rt_frame.glsl:2-7` includes the scene ABI, diagnostics, lighting, dielectric common/transport and atmosphere. The frame traces one primary scene ray then calls `shadePrimary` (`rt_frame.glsl:42-60`). It also integrates fire volume separately (`rt_frame.glsl:60-62`); that volume/reflection work is distinct from direct-light visibility samples.

For an opaque primary receiver, `shadeOpaquePrimary` calls `shadeOpaqueDirect` and may add one ordinary/reflection bounce (`rt_dielectric_common.glsl:278-329`). The direct helper batches up to four enabled visibility samples: local light, its optional second area point, sky, optional second sky point (`rt_lighting.glsl:1142-1219`). The second local/sky samples are enabled only when `dualVisibility` is true and their contribution gates pass (`rt_lighting.glsl:1208-1212`). On this path `dualVisibility=maxWorkload`, where max workload means `workloadPreset >= 1.5` (`rt_dielectric_common.glsl:297-310`). Thus direct-light visibility has a static ceiling of 2 samples when dual is false and 4 when dual is true, before fire-emitter samples.

The same opaque direct helper invokes fire direct lighting. Its loop is capped at two active emitters; each surviving emitter contributes one visibility sample, or two if `dualVisibility` is true (`rt_lighting.glsl:1037-1085`; ABI active capacity 2 at `RtSceneAbi.def:9-10`). Therefore an opaque primary has a source ceiling of 2+N samples at non-dual workload or 4+2N at dual workload, where N is the number of uploaded active emitters that also pass the shader's receiver-facing term gate. This is at most 4 / 8 respectively for N<=2; it is not a claim those sites execute for every pixel.

Opaque terminal secondary receivers use `shadeOpaqueSecondary -> shadeOpaqueDirect(..., false, false, ...)` (`rt_lighting.glsl:1258-1298`): at most one eligible local sample, one eligible sky sample, and one eligible fire sample per active emitter (N<=2), hence static ceiling 2+N (maximum 4). Emissive, miss, and water terminal cases return before direct visibility work (`rt_lighting.glsl:1263-1274`). Ordinary non-light-aware bounce hits use ambient only and launch no direct-light query; the light-aware mirror bounce uses one local-light query only when strength is >0.001, plus fire direct samples with dual visibility disabled (`rt_lighting.glsl:939-1000`).

Thin-water primary shading has its own interface visibility: local is gated by `localStrength > 0.001`, sky is queried once, and fire direct visibility is invoked once per eligible active emitter, with dual visibility false (`rt_dielectric_common.glsl:90-276`, especially 200-244). Its source ceiling is 2+N; the local sample may be zero. Do not transfer this thin-water interface-lighting count to generic dielectric interfaces. Generic production dielectric transport has a Mobile budget of four transmission interfaces (`rt_dielectric_transport.glsl:424-430, 507-508, 555-585, 667-672`); those interfaces compute Fresnel/refraction/TIR/throughput and trace continuation rays, not the thin-water local/sky/fire highlights. Instead, the production path has at most two terminal opaque shading sites per invocation: one opaque reflection terminal (`rt_dielectric_transport.glsl:444-484`) and one opaque transmission terminal (`rt_dielectric_transport.glsl:517-552`). Each such terminal calls `shadeTerminalOpaqueEmissive`, so each can incur up to 2+N direct samples; misses, water, and dielectric reflection terminals take the non-direct branch. Thus the static direct-light ceiling for this path is at most 4+2N across the two opaque terminals (maximum 8 when N=2), independent of the four-interface continuation budget. The actual paths and active variant remain runtime-dependent.

## Current zero-work gates and fire population

Direct-light sample count is gated, not fixed. Local samples require `localStrength > 0.001` and a positive diffuse or specular term; sky samples require positive sky gain and a positive diffuse or specular term (`rt_lighting.glsl:1196-1212`). Fire samples require a nonzero emitter identity, strength >0.001, and positive diffuse or enabled analytic-specular contribution (`rt_lighting.glsl:1046-1075`). Secondary opaque shading disables dual visibility and analytic fire specular (`rt_lighting.glsl:1280-1282`). `visibilityMask` is one ray-query initialization per enabled visibility sample (`rt_lighting.glsl:846-869`); generic-transmission builds route that sample through bounded transmittance, so a native counter should distinguish sample initializations from candidate/continuation work (`rt_lighting.glsl:871-879`).

Simulation owns one configured opening-torch emitter (`GameSimulation.h:120-123`) and updates it from held-light transforms, flame strength, fuel and current zone (`GameSimulation.cpp:658-667`). Upload selection requires matching zone, strength* fuel >0.0001, and distance <=24m; it retains at most the two nearest candidates, tie-sorted by stable ID (`FireEmitterBuffer.cpp:91-159`). Mobile fire quality selects 4 volume steps / 1 reflection sample (`FireEmitterBuffer.cpp:44-49`), which is separate from direct visibility. The renderer selects Mobile quality unless water quality is High, and packs the simulation emitters (`PresentableTinyRtScene.cpp:5150-5167`). Thus ordinary opening source setup offers one torch emitter; exact per-frame active count still depends on flame/fuel, zone, and distance.

The production reward lantern is an additional synthetic fire emitter only when `productionLanternVisible` is true (inspection override, available reward phase, or claimed reward; `PresentableTinyRtScene.cpp:4955-4971`). It is packed with strength 0.78 and appended into the two-slot active set, possibly replacing the last selected slot (`PresentableTinyRtScene.cpp:5171-5200`). That is a reward-lantern world object, distinct from the ordinary held torch emitter. The source does not prove it is visible/active in an “ordinary opening” frame or that a particular live capture uploaded one versus two slots; inspect the per-frame selected IDs/count to know.

## What needs runtime instrumentation

To turn these ceilings into evidence, count: per-frame primary opaque / thin-water receiver invocations and generic dielectric continuation interfaces plus reflection/transmission terminal classes; enabled local/sky/fire visibility samples at terminal receivers; dual-workload flag; uploaded emitter active count and stable IDs; and query continuation/candidate work separately where generic transmittance is used. No existing source census establishes dynamic samples-per-receiver, average workload, or a phone result.
