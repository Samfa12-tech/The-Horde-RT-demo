and protection; `SwordCombat` retains shared phases, timestamped input consumption,
immediate riposte, target ordering and existing route rules. Native UI publishes
preferences and monotonic skip/replay commands; JNI does not mutate gameplay directly.

| Behavior | Initial value / policy |

| Existing dodge | 0.20 s displacement, 0.90 m travel and 0.55 s cooldown retained |

| Optional teaching slowdown | 0.45 gameplay-time scale during an unlearned teaching interval; off by default |
| Prompt completion fade | 120 ms; reduced-motion mode removes the fade |

These are reversible development tuning values, not owner feel acceptance.
Baseline dodge protection needs no upgrade. Rejected/cooldown/spammed inputs cannot
restart or extend immunity. Skeleton melee and Keeper lightning use the same damage
resolution policy. Pause, focus/lifecycle synchronization, death, retry, reset and
checkpoint import cancel transient protection. Animation cannot create immunity.

Repel uses existing collision/support resolution, stops at blocked motion and rejects
support-height changes or invalid ground. It cannot deliberately force a fall or
cross a wall. Looking and defensive inputs remain available; transient run intent
is cancelled. An accepted nonfatal hit cancels an old cast before the new warning
cycle. The lethal third hit cancels repel/discharge/warnings and retains ordered
Keeper defeat, failed-torch state and reward admission.

Live applications select the new rules. Explicit historical showcase imports and
legacy fixtures retain one-hit skeletons and their original encounter behavior.
Both full modern and historical waterfall-route test registrations check exact
health, IDs, placement and route constraints. This does not change released artifacts.

## Teaching, input priority and time policy

The existing encounters supply the lesson. Safe practice ignores incoming health
loss until both actual parry and protected-dodge results are learned, or until the
owner skips/disables the lesson. A miss is counted without extending the one-second
guard. Movement and looking remain available outside the existing Keeper reveal
hold. Retry retains learned/skip state; explicit replay resets the opening lesson.
Preferences are settings only: no campaign slots, progression unlocks or save system.

The banner distinguishes hollow wind-up, anticipatory **PRESS PARRY**, filled
**PARRY WINDOW ACTIVE**, directional **DODGE NOW**, and recovery cues. Bounded progress,
text/shape and existing admitted sounds provide alternatives to colour. The actual
parry window is not widened to match the prompt. Keeper charging refers to the
existing staff glow; no dedicated casting or directional dodge animation is claimed.
No new lights, sound assets, UI framework, flashing effect or asset acquisition.

The optional slowdown scales one fixed-step gameplay delta before movement, contact,
defence, enemy clocks, equipment, animation and rewards. Input still arrives and is
consumed on real 60 Hz ticks; timestamped edges are not backdated or remapped to a
different clock. Enter/exit, skip and pause are tested. Prompt fading is presentation
only and cannot extend defence.

An accepted attack wins simultaneous attack/parry input; the parry edge is consumed
and discarded. Existing downward wind-up/active input can buffer one upward
continuation. There is no new recovery buffer, directional combo system or wider
parry window. Dodge retains its existing ability to be requested alongside actions.

## Contact investigation and unresolved decision

The unchanged forgiving pulse/cone rule was reproduced against the actual imported
player/Grip, blade mesh and skinned target. Frontal pulse17 at 1.28 m showed a
275.347 mm blade/mesh gap; the −15 degree case showed 234.658 mm. The ordinary moving
approach reproduced the historical 215.684 / 17.5033 mm gaps. This is not evidence
for a universal pulse delay or a wider parry window.

The final real-mesh witness records 24 discrete queries and 1,125,143 triangle pairs.
Coarse, maximum-radius and percentile capsule experiments produced misleading
contact or inadequate coverage and were rejected. A diagnostic inward-pose experiment
uses the existing grip/retraction path but is **not selected by runtime simulation**.

The conservative 24-joint, 25 mm capsule experiment measured 84 poses: 11 accepted
active samples, zero accepts beyond blade-plus-bone radii in those samples, 20 mesh-near
misses and five root-cone-only pulse samples, including two moving-target samples.
Nearest-joint labels are not vertex-weight classification. This is discrete sampling,
not a continuous sweep, complete mesh coverage, runtime admission or device pass.
The experimental callback rejects missing/dead skeleton IDs, preserves per-cut
deduplication and reserves Keeper ID2 for the caller; gameplay does not select it.

**Pending owner decision:** require conservative body contact and tune the tighter
approach/aim requirement, treating weapon/glancing mesh intersections as misses; or
retain forgiving contact while completing dodge/teaching and explicitly defer contact.
The question remains unanswered. No experimental contact rule has been silently enabled.
If tighter contact is selected, measured immutable runtime pose data, occlusion and
moving-target integration still need completion and validation within WP2a.
