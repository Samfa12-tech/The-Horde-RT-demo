## 2. Separate texture-fetch cost from material classification

These are two different hypotheses with separate gates:

- **Fetch/footprint probe:** hold decoded material values, ray directions, reflection selection, ray/sample budgets, lighting and geometry fixed while isolating texture traffic. Prove those invariants in the diagnostic design. Simply replacing textured stone with a flat material changes the workload and cannot attribute a speedup to texture bandwidth.
- **Classification/transport probe:** measure the actual receiver coverage and cost of the existing material policy before changing it. [World ARM decode](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/8717112f16b010460ae4045d4b406a4f7f253b98/shaders/raytracing/include/rt_hit_decode.glsl#L283-L335) maps reflectivity from `clamp(1 - roughness, .05, .96)`. Combined with [reflective > .38 selection](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/8717112f16b010460ae4045d4b406a4f7f253b98/shaders/raytracing/include/rt_dielectric_common.glsl#L312-L348), roughness below .62 can select reflection-oriented handling. [Light-aware bounce shading](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/8717112f16b010460ae4045d4b406a4f7f253b98/shaders/raytracing/include/rt_lighting.glsl#L1000-L1061) now includes visibility and fire lighting. Old texture data can therefore interact with later expensive lighting; this is a source-backed hypothesis, not measured pixel coverage or a proven regression cause.

Any later classification change must preserve important mirror, wet, metal, glass, water, player and held-item reflections, report changed radiance and obtain quality acceptance. Do not combine it with a fetch probe, change direction/ray budgets without declaring a separate quality experiment, or repeat Lower unchanged.

### Future experiment: light visibility and bounded many-light sampling

**Owner direction recorded 8 October 2026:** retain this research as future experimental work. It is not an additional 1.6.2 shipping gate, an instruction to start a device run, or an adopted renderer change. Preserve the historical-investigation priority above and select one bounded hypothesis at a time.

**Research basis.** A separate visibility ray toward each sampled light is a normal direct-lighting method; one ray cannot independently establish visibility to arbitrary different lights. [Arm's Vulkan shadow tutorial](https://learn.arm.com/learning-paths/mobile-graphics-and-gaming/ray_tracing/rt07_shadows/) illustrates the basic per-light approach. For larger light populations, the [original ReSTIR paper](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2020-07_spatiotemporal-reservoir-resampling-real-time-ray-tracing-dynamic-direct) describes spatiotemporal reservoir resampling to select useful light samples. [RTXDI integration guidance](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/blob/main/Doc/Integration.md) shows the scene-data and sampling integration involved. [Epic's MegaLights documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/megalights-in-unreal-engine) gives a production example of stochastic direct lighting with a bounded sampling budget. These are technique references, not evidence of a Horde phone speedup, mobile compatibility, or a drop-in solution.

**Starting point to recheck.** The [audited shader](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/abe05aec40d6df654ad548a6b2d9360c71c9ea65/shaders/raytracing/include/rt_lighting.glsl#L980-L1278) already shares each receiver/light visibility result across diffuse and specular terms, skips zero diffuse-and-specular contributions, and uses one shadow sample per contributing light at Current. [Fire admission](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/abe05aec40d6df654ad548a6b2d9360c71c9ea65/src/vulkan/raytracing/FireEmitterBuffer.cpp#L132-L183) is bounded by zone, camera distance and a four-active-fire capacity; the runtime separately appends the visible reward-lantern emitter within that capacity. [Mist](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/abe05aec40d6df654ad548a6b2d9360c71c9ea65/shaders/raytracing/include/rt_atmosphere.glsl) already reuses midpoint source visibility across density samples. The four-fire limit is a current demo bound, not a complete town-lighting solution.

1. **Attribute current cost first.** Count actual launched queries by light, receiver/path, scene and quality tier: opaque primary, reflected/refracted receivers, glass and mist. Separate scene rays from analytic fire/mist integration steps and static shader call sites. Pair diagnostic counts with appropriately isolated GPU timings and quantify instrumentation overhead. Hold the scene, camera, backend, resolution, materials and quality fixed; use warm, thermally comparable phone observations and retain unavailable-counter gaps. Select a candidate only when the evidence identifies a useful target.
2. **Test small exact eliminations before new architecture.** Investigate finite zero-radiance guards and zero-diffuse guards on diffuse-only reflected-light paths. Expect limited opportunity, not a promised meaningful gain. In Higher shadows, test reuse only when both moon visibility segments are genuinely identical in receiver, origin, direction, distance and mask; preserve distinct finale-aperture samples. This cannot save a second sky query at Current, which already uses one. Never reuse visibility across different receivers, moving shadow boundaries, reflection/refraction paths or unrelated lights merely because they are nearby.
3. **Evaluate per-receiver whole-light relevance as an explicit approximation.** Current distance attenuation never becomes exactly zero. A range or contribution threshold must therefore bound omitted light energy, including the aggregate error from multiple lights, and be labelled a quality experiment. Reject negligible whole contributions before traversal; do not keep their illumination while silently assuming a skipped shadow ray is unoccluded. Define stable transitions to avoid popping and validate consistency in secondary views and mist, colored overlap, motion, extinction and gameplay-critical lighting. Keep the same-quality baseline and a reversible fallback.
4. **Consider many-light sampling only when a measured town workload needs it.** Compare a bounded selection/reservoir prototype against simple relevance culling at an explicitly fixed visibility-ray budget. Account for candidate selection, history/reservoir buffers, geometry/material inputs, motion and disocclusion handling, denoising, bandwidth, memory and total frame cost on the intended phone. Test moving lights, occluders, fire, water/glass and difficult secondary views. A fixed ray budget alone does not establish better image quality or lower total cost. Do not import RTXDI or add a temporal renderer solely because the demo has four fire slots.

**Keep/reject gate:** use the experiment-card and sustained-evidence rules below. Require a repeatable useful net benefit with acceptable motion/lighting quality; otherwise stop and retain the result. No measured saving, default change, implementation schedule or release commitment is established here.

## 7. Evidence budget, decisions and sustained acceptance

For each selected future hypothesis, write a short experiment card before execution: exact question, immutable baseline/candidate, changed mechanism, predicted observable, protected quality, device/time/run budget, run order, stop conditions and keep/reject/defer rule. One hypothesis at a time. Begin with the smallest diagnostic that can discriminate it; escalate only on useful evidence. Stop at the agreed budget, failed invariants, unacceptable quality or no repeatable net benefit. Preserve failures and uncertainty; do not tune or extend runs merely to obtain a favorable result.

Match hardware/driver/backend, camera/route and loaded content, internal/output dimensions, material and lighting controls, build/compiler policy, timing definitions, power connection and thermal conditions. Alternate repeated control/candidate runs; include fresh-process and sustained observations. Battery temperature alone does not establish matched GPU clocks. Record missing counters and endpoint gaps.

Report active CPU stages, GPU AS/update, trace/shading and output/upscale/transfer where valid, plus end-to-end presentation/pacing, per-zone median and tails, missed deadlines, quality captures, memory and thermal/power context. Measure diagnostic overhead; acceptance uses admitted Shipping artifacts. Native CPU-observed cycles include waits; GPU intervals include multiple components. Do not add CPU and GPU durations, subtract unrelated medians, or turn the wrong metric into an FPS claim.

Keep correctness/build success, visual acceptance and sustained performance acceptance visibly separate. The 21 August policy change to [descriptive bands and investigation thresholds](https://github.com/Samfa12-tech/The-Horde-RT-demo/blob/92a16f355b1e5eefe62ccac05e666be1e84e0fde/docs/CURRENT_DEVELOPMENT_BASELINE_VALIDATION_2026-08-21.md#L123-L133) did not prove the target was met.
